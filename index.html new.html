Professional Introduction
Who We Are

We are K. Gayatri and Gouthami, graduate students at Franklin University pursuing advanced studies in Business Analytics. Our work bridges data science and public health — transforming complex county-level datasets into actionable insights for policymakers and healthcare professionals.

Our Capstone project investigates adult obesity rates across Ohio's 88 counties, applying four rigorous analytical methods — K-Means clustering, logistic regression, decision trees, and linear regression — to uncover the structural drivers behind one of the nation's most pressing public health challenges.

We are passionate about using analytics to drive evidence-based decisions that make data accessible, interpretable, and immediately useful for the communities that need it most.

Capstone Story
Adult Obesity Rates
Across Ohio Counties

A data-driven investigation into the county-level health factors driving adult obesity variation across all 88 Ohio counties — and what analytics reveals for smarter public health action.

BUSA-695 · Franklin University · 2023 CDC PLACES Data

Why Ohio's Obesity Crisis Demanded Analytics

Obesity affects millions of U.S. adults and is directly linked to heart disease, diabetes, and stroke — costing billions in healthcare expenditure annually. Ohio's 88 counties show dramatic variation: from 33.2% in Delaware County to 46.3% in Ashtabula — a 13-point gap within a single state. Our research asked: What county-level factors most strongly predict adult obesity rates, and can machine learning classify counties into distinct risk profiles?

Methods Applied
Four-Model Analytical Framework

We applied a multi-method approach, each model answering a specific research sub-question about Ohio county obesity patterns using 2023 CDC PLACES data.

01

Correlation Analysis

Heatmap analysis of all 7 variables revealed relationship strengths and detected critical multicollinearity: High_BP ↔ Diabetes = 0.89.

02

K-Means Clustering

Unsupervised learning grouped all 88 counties into 3 risk profiles. High-risk counties averaged 6 percentage points higher obesity than low-risk.

03

Logistic Regression

Binary classification of high vs. low obesity counties. Physical inactivity (0.90) and high blood pressure (0.85) emerged as top predictors.

04

Decision Tree

Visual classification splitting first on No_Leisure, then High_BP — designed to be usable by non-technical public health stakeholders.

05

Linear Regression

Predicts continuous county obesity rates. R² = 0.489, F-statistic = 12.90, p < 0.001. High BP was the strongest individual predictor.

Key Insights
Recommendations for Public Health Action

Five evidence-based recommendations derived from our multi-model analysis — designed to be actionable for Ohio county health departments and policymakers.

01

Expand Physical Activity Programs

Target Cluster 2 (high-risk) counties with investment in recreational infrastructure. Physical inactivity is the #1 predictor of high obesity classification (coefficient: 0.90).

HIGHEST IMPACT
02

Blood Pressure Prevention Programs

Implement community BP screening as obesity prevention. High_BP has a regression coefficient of 0.672 and correlates at r = 0.649 with obesity rates.

HIGH IMPACT
03

Decision Tree as a Policy Tool

Non-technical stakeholders can use the decision tree to classify at-risk counties without statistical expertise — splitting on No_Leisure first, then High_BP.

ACCESSIBILITY
04

Address Food Insecurity

Deploy food access programs in counties showing combined food insecurity and high obesity — particularly in rural southeastern Ohio where clusters overlap.

EQUITY FOCUS
05

Annual Model Refresh

Update predictive models each year as new CDC PLACES data is released — enabling health departments to track whether interventions are measurably reducing risk.
